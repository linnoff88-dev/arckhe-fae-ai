MODULE 2.3

📊 Map the process

A diagram explains in 5 seconds what text can’t explain in 5 paragraphs. Here, you learn to draw the flow using minimal notation, compare as-is with to-be, find bottlenecks, map decisions, mark where AI comes in, and validate everything with the client before building.

🔣 Simple flow notation

You don’t need full BPMN. Three symbols handle 95% of processes: rectangle for one step, diamond for one decision and arrow for the direction of the flow. Simple enough for anyone to understand.

💡 Practical tip

Start with pencil and paper. A hand-drawn diagram made in 10 minutes is worth more than a perfect BPMN that nobody finishes.

🔄 As-is vs. to-be

Draw two flows side by side: the as-is (how it works today, full of manual steps) and the to-be (what it will look like with AI, streamlined). The visual contrast is the most convincing proof of value — the client sees the steps that disappear.

✗ As-is (current)

  • ✗Too many manual steps and screen changes
  • ✗Waiting and rework along the way
  • ✗Long cycle time, high cost

✓ To-be (future state)

  • ✓Manual steps eliminated or combined
  • ✓AI handles classification, extraction, and generation
  • ✓Cycle time in seconds, low cost

🧱 Find bottlenecks and rework

With the as-is mapped out, the problems become obvious. Bottleneck is the slowest step, which limits the whole process. Rework is a correction loop that could have been avoided. Both are the areas with the highest returns.

1

Mark the slowest step

The bottleneck

That’s where the queue forms. Speeding it up speeds up the entire process.

2

Look for arrows that loop back

The rework

Correction loops indicate that something was done wrong earlier. Fix the cause, not the symptom.

3

Find the wait times

Dead time

Waiting for approval or for someone else is usually the biggest cause of a long cycle time.

💡 Practical tip

Addressing the bottleneck yields more than optimizing any other step. Improving a step that isn't the bottleneck doesn't speed anything up.

🌿 Decision points and branches

Every process branches at some point: approved or rejected, type A, B, or C. These decision points need to be mapped carefully—each branch must have a clear destination, and the conditions must be mutually exclusive.

⚠️ Attention

A decision without a complete exit branch becomes a bug in the automation. Make sure that todo the path has a destination — including "none of the options".

🤖 Where AI fits in the diagram

Not every step needs AI. Mark only the nodes where it really shines in the to-be: language (classify, extract, summarize, generate) and diffuse judgment. Fixed rules? Use regular logic — it's cheaper and more reliable.

✓ AI shines at

  • ✓Classify free text (emails, tickets)
  • ✓Extract data from unstructured documents
  • ✓Summarize, translate, and generate text
  • ✓Making decisions with diffuse context

✗ Don’t use AI to

  • ✗Calculations one formula can handle
  • ✗Fixed rules like "if A, then B"
  • ✗Move data from one field to another
  • ✗Decisions that require exact precision

🏷️ Mark the intelligent nodes

In the to-be diagram, highlight the AI steps with a distinct color or icon. That makes it clear where the automation’s “brain” is—and where it’s just plumbing.

✅ Validate the diagram with the client

Before writing a line of automation, sit down with someone who knows the process and validate the diagram. Fixing a design takes minutes; fixing a finished automation takes days. The right question is "is this right?" — not "shall I build it?".

🔍 Validation plan

  • •Go through step by step with whoever executes it.
  • •Ask about exceptions that were missing from the design.
  • •Confirm the to-be: does the proposed future state make sense?
  • •Define the scope: validated diagram = agreement on what will be built.

💡 Practical tip

Treat the validated diagram as a visual contract. It aligns expectations and protects you from change requests that "were always in scope."
