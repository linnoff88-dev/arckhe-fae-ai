MODULE 2.1

🎯 Find the 10-hour task

Every company has invisible tasks that consume hours each month without anyone adding up the cost. In this module, you’ll learn to spot these opportunities, separate what’s worth automating from what isn’t, and choose the first process based on clear criteria—not guesswork.

🗺️ Where the opportunities are

Automation opportunities aren't hidden in exotic places — they're in plain sight, in work nobody wants to do. Three areas account for almost all of them: routines that repeat, bottlenecks where the work piles up and copy-paste between systems that don’t communicate.

✓ Where to look

  • ✓Reports put together by hand every week
  • ✓Data copied from one system to another
  • ✓Standardized emails answered one by one
  • ✓Spreadsheets updated manually every day

✗ Where it tends to be a waste of time

  • ✗Strategic decisions that change every time
  • ✗Tasks done once a year
  • ✗Negotiations that require reading people
  • ✗Processes with no stable rules

💡 Practical tip

Ask anyone on the team: "what part of your day do you hate doing?" The answer almost always points straight to a routine that can be automated.

🚦 Signs of an automatable task

Not every tedious task is worth automating. The ones that are have clear signals: stable rules, high volume, structured data, and predictable results. The more signals that line up, the stronger the candidate.

🎯 The 4 signs that matter

  • •Deterministic: the same input always produces the same output.
  • •Repetitive: happens many times a day, week, or month.
  • •Rule-based: you can write the "how to do it" in clear steps.
  • •Tedious: low cognitive value, high attention cost.

📊 Candidate thermometer

4 signals present  → excellent candidate
2–3 signals       → worth investigating
0–1 signal        → leave it for later

🎤 Interview the process owner

The process described in the manual is almost never the real process. To uncover the truth, you talk to the person who does the task every day — and how you ask makes all the difference. Use a interview sequence that reveals hidden steps, shortcuts, and pain points.

1

"Show me how you do it"

Live demonstration

Ask the person to perform the task while you observe. The informal shortcuts only show up in practice.

2

"What annoys you most here?"

Pain map

Frustration points directly to the bottleneck. Where it hurts, there’s value in automating.

3

"When does this go wrong?"

Hunt for exceptions

The exceptions you don't uncover now will break the automation later.

⚠️ Attention

Never ask “how should it be?” That gets you theory, not practice. Ask “how does it work today, really?”—warts and all.

📋 Task inventory and frequency

Without an inventory, you prioritize by intuition—and intuition gets it wrong. List every repetitive task with its frequency e time per run. Multiplying reveals where time really leaks over the course of the year.

📊 Illustrative inventory

💡 Practical tip

The formula is simple: time per run × annual frequency = total time. Small, frequent tasks almost always beat large, rare ones.

📐 Effort × impact matrix

With the inventory in hand, you need to prioritize. The effort × impact matrix compares how difficult something is to automate with how much the automation delivers. The golden corner is high impact, low effort — always start there.

✓ Prioritize

  • ✓Quick wins: high return, little effort
  • ✓Large projects only after proving value

✗ Avoid

  • ✗High effort, low return
  • ✗Start with the most complex process

🥇 Choose the first process

Your first project defines your reputation. It needs to be small enough to deliver quickly, visible enough so everyone notices and low risk to avoid causing damage if it fails.

🎯 First project criteria

  • •Small: delivery in days, not months.
  • •Visible: the benefit goes to whoever makes the decisions.
  • •Low risk: if it fails, no one stops working.
  • •Measurable: you can prove the before and after.

💡 Practical tip

Think of the first project as a demonstration, not as the final solution. It exists to prove that automation works and pave the way for what comes next.
